I listened to an interview with Tsunehiro Uno about his latest [book](https://www.kodansha.co.jp/book/products/0000404327), and he talked about the texture of one's connection to society, as something in desparate need of nuance in today's world of social media-fueled identity politics. The word he used was *tezawari*, which literally means "hand feel". 

The phrase brings a subjectivity to the idea of connection -- a tactile quality requires a body to make contact with it -- that made me put words to why I've been enjoying my recent time in public spaces so much. (It's not just because there are less people, which is the typical reason that Parisians give!) 

In the past week, I had a drink along [Paris Plages](https://www.paris.fr/evenements/tout-savoir-sur-paris-plages-2026-114591), caught a(nother) film at [Cinema en plein air](https://www.lavillette.com/manifestations/cinema-en-plein-air-26/) and walked among sunbathers at Parc des Buttes-Chaumont. All of these sites are free of charge. All bodies are welcome and you don't need to be a certain type to feel "in". There are some rules and expectations to maintain harmony but there is little policing happening. Each person has a right to be there and to enjoy it, just by virtue of their presence. 

These qualities culminate in a certain sense of acceptance and belonging -- a connection to the larger whole that the world online cannot offer.

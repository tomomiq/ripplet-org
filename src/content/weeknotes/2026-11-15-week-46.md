We're going back and forth between crisp autumn light and dreary winter greys in Paris.

![](/weeknotes-images/IMG_1875.jpg)
![](/weeknotes-images/IMG_1864.jpg)


I went to Grand Palais to see the **[Cezanne et nous](https://www.grandpalais.fr/en/program/cezanne-and-us)** exhibition. It was a playful, affectionate curation of Cezanne's works alongside those by many artists who were captivated by him, both in his time and much after. 

Cezanne is my favorite 19th century artist. There's something confident and impersonal about his style that spoke to me even as a kid, as if the work wasn't about him but what he wanted others to see. This exhibition showed just how much his work shaped the eyes of fellow artists, something I'd never given much thought to. And I was surprised to notice in myself a strong sense of "Cezanne et moi"--that I was somewhat territorial, even jealous that they'd acted on their inspiration while I remained an admirer.

On Saturday, I went to see **Stefan Sagmeister's [Finally, Something Good](https://www.gaite-lyrique.net/agenda/expositions/2026-10-15-finally-something-good/)** at Gaîté Lyrique, and watched his 2016 film, [Happy Film](https://www.thehappyfilm.org/), too. In contrast to the Cezanne show, this is a body of work that's about him. 

I think Stefan Sagmeister is perhaps the most accessible, contemporaneous example of designer-as-artist-as-entrepreneur. Over three decades, he's maintained a practice that combines commercial viability with artistic autonomy. Or at least that's how it looks from the outside, and I'd argue that this perception is a crucial part of what makes his practice so compelling.

I'm not so much moved by his work as I am fascinated by the practice he's built around it. It's cool that this show entered my orbits as I'm re-kindling my own design practice.

On Sunday, a friend came over to start sketching a small publication we're putting together. It'll be a small handbook-ish thing for collaboration as a creative endeavor--a set of tools to develop greater literacy around the self as collaborator. 

We spent a few hours exploring ideas, sharing inspirations, and landing on a skeleton. Fun times! We'll have another session next week, to get to a prototype they can bring to a conference.

Speaking of food experiences, I have a new favorite fruit: **green figs**. Tasted fresh for the first time in Greece, after our Airbhb host plucked a generous bagful from his backyard. I bought home dried figs (long gone now!) and a jar of fig jam. As often happens when you notice something for the first time, I started seeing it everywhere. I spied fresh green ones at a fruit-and-veg shop in my neighborhood. Biscuits with figs. Amuse-bouches with caramelized fig on mascarpone. It's been fun.

I sat down with my Kindle as I was winding down one night, and it opened on a Japanese poem that had spoken to me a while back. On a whim, I decided to try my hand at translating it into English, as a way of grapping with the material. I really enjoyed the worthsmithing process, and feel pretty satisfied with how it turned out! 

The poem is called "Your own sensitivity, at least" (自分の感受性くらい) by the postwar poet Noriko Ibaragi. She wrote it as a message to herself. 

**Your own sensitivity, at least**

Don't blame other people <br>
for your heart drying up, crumbling. <br>
When you yourself aren't watering it. 

Don't blame your friends<br>
that you're becoming a grump. <br>
Which one of us is losing our grace, anyway?

Don't blame your family<br>
because you're irritated again. <br>
It was I, who was clumsy in all of the things.

Don't blame your day-to-day<br>
for your youthful aspirations fading. <br>
It was but a frail ambition from the start... 

Don't blame the changing times <br>
for all that isn't working. <br>
The abandonment of the last glimmer of dignity.

Your own sensitivity, at least! <br>
Protect it yourself.<br>
You fool.
